Case study · Vennbee · 2026

Moderation that keeps a new social network clean without banning Harshit.

Apple won't ship a social app until every kind of user content can be filtered, reported and acted on. When we started, Vennbee only checked posts. I led the work to cover everything else, without treating people's real names as offensive.

01

Only posts were covered.

Our first moderation checked posts: captions, images, video and audio. Everything else a person could type, like names, bios, community names and links, went public as soon as it was saved, and not every surface had a way to report it.

Before submitting to the App Store, I listed every place someone could type something, and checked which were covered. Most weren't. That list became the plan.

02

What it had to get right.

  • Real names aren't slurs. Harshit contains a swear word. So do plenty of surnames. A filter that bans people for their own names is worse than no filter.
  • Signups can't break. Moderation can't become the reason someone can't create an account.
  • Cheap enough to check everything. Every name and bio, every time it changes, on a pre-revenue budget.
  • Reuse what we have. No second pipeline: the same workers and queues the app already runs.

03

The principles.

AA person decides the borderline cases.

Clear-cut content is handled automatically. Anything ambiguous goes to a human, because most of what an automatic filter catches in names is a false positive.

TradeoffReview takes people's time. It's still cheaper than wrongly banning someone on day one.

BConsistent everywhere.

The same standard applies to a name, a bio, a post or an image, so content doesn't pass in one place and fail in another.

TradeoffWe passed on cheaper options that would have used different rules for different content.

CNever say the word back.

When something is rejected, the message says so without repeating what was offensive.

MistakeAn early version echoed the offending word in the error. It was fixed the same day.

DBuilt for review, not just detection.

Every report and decision lands in a dashboard with an audit trail, so a human can see what happened and why.

WhyModeration that nobody can inspect is moderation nobody can trust or fix.

04

What I learned.

  • Count the surfaces first. The list of every place a person can type something was the most useful document in the project.
  • Every exception is a decision. Allowing one name is easy; making sure that rule doesn't open a hole elsewhere is the real work.
  • Plan for your vendors to change. AI models get retired. Moderation that depends on one needs alerts for when it stops working.

Vennbee is live, so this write-up stays at the level of principles. How the checks work stays private, on purpose.
