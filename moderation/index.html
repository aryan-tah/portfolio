Case study · Vennbee · March to November 2026

Moderation that keeps a new social network clean without banning Harshit.

Apple won't ship a social app until every kind of user content can be filtered, reported and acted on. Vennbee only moderated posts. Names, bios, spaces and links went public the instant they were written. I rebuilt moderation to cover all of it, cheaply, and without punishing people for their own names.

01

Only posts were safe.

In March I built a worker that checked every post: captions, images, video frames and audio. That covered the obvious surface. It missed everything else.

Profile names, bios, space names and link titles had no checks at all, and there was no word filter anywhere. And App Review expects a way to report every kind of content, which we didn't have either. Before submitting, I wrote down every surface where someone could type something and checked which ones were covered. Most weren't.

02

The constraints.

  • Names can't wait. A post can sit hidden for 30 seconds while it's checked. A display name can't; it's someone's identity. Names had to be checked during the request.
  • Real names aren't slurs. Harshit contains “shit”. Dickens contains another word. A filter that bans people for their own names is worse than no filter.
  • A vendor outage can't block signups. If the moderation model is down, people still need to be able to create an account.
  • Cheap enough to check everything. Every name and bio, every time it changes.
  • Don't build a second system. Reuse the queues and workers we already had.

03

Five decisions.

ATwo tiers: block a few words, flag the rest.

A short list of unambiguous terms is blocked outright. Everything else that looks suspicious goes through and is flagged for review. Most of what a word list catches is a false positive; a person should decide those.

CostA borderline name can be live for a while before someone reviews it. I'll take that over banning Harshit.

BFail open, then catch up.

If the model is down, the write goes through and is checked again later. A sweep finds anything that never got checked and runs it again.

CaughtA bug in how jobs were named silently dropped every fail-open check. The sweep is what made it visible, and it's why the sweep exists.

COne scale for everything.

Text, images, video and audio are scored on the same scale by the same vendor. Two different systems could reject a bio at 9:00 and pass the same bio at 9:01.

CostWe gave up a free moderation endpoint. It would have saved about $40 per million checks, and it couldn't handle a burst of signups anyway.

DStrikes follow the person, not the profile.

Vennbee lets you have several profiles. Strikes count against the account, so starting a fresh profile isn't an escape. Repeated strikes lead to a temporary restriction, not a ban.

FixedPosts and names first kept separate strike counts, which quietly doubled everyone's chances. One ledger now.

ENever say the word back.

When something is rejected, the error tells you it was rejected, not which word did it.

MistakeMy first version echoed the offending word in the error message. It was gone the same day.

05

What went wrong, and what I'd keep.

  • The model will change under you. Our vendor retired a model and video moderation broke overnight. Pin versions and alert when a check starts failing.
  • Every exception is a hole. An allowlist entry meant for real names let a crude username through. That's what pushed the design to two tiers: the word list proposes, the model decides.
  • Count the surfaces first. The list of every place a person can type something was the most useful document in the project. Three report types were still missing from the server when I checked it against that list.

This write-up covers what Vennbee's moderation does and why. Thresholds, prompts and word lists stay private, on purpose.
